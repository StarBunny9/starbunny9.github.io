Project Description

Puzzle Pals is a game made to be displayed in the central library of Enschede and is made in collaboration with children from the International School Twente. They all made monster drawings and came up with various super powers for their monsters.

Puzzle Pals delivers an opportunity to explore the imagination of children and casually challenges the problem-solving skills of curious minds that are open to a short, positive, and brightening experience while exploring the library.

My Contributions
Design Direction

Making design decisions based on the requirements, scope, target audience, how the product is going to be used, and when it's not so clear what the best solutions are.

Since our team consisted of three programmers, one designer and two artists, I decided that this was a good opportunity to make some steps in learning proper game design. This also balanced out the roles within the team.

The general design direction of the game as whole was mostly decided by the team as a whole, since everybody has to work on it and wants their vision to be included. So what exactly was my role in this game's design process? This mostly boils down to aligning and communicating different ideas with team members and external parties to create a cohesive experience for the children and anyone who plays the game. This also includes making decisions based on the requirements, scope, target audience, how the product is going to be used, and when it's not so clear what the best solutions are.

One of these decisions was on how the camera interactions should work. Internally, we preferred the player to be able to have a freely orbiting camera. But we noticed during playtests with the children, that they really struggled getting a grip on how to use such a camera systems. Considering the fact that the final product is supposed to be a short and lighthearted experience, we chose to continue with a static third person camera, that only moves when the player enters a new area, based on where important gameplay aspects are. This decision reflected positively from our playtests, where children struggled considerably less to find their way around the levels. Allowing the player to learn a freely orbiting camera system would likely have been a better choice if the average playtime of a user would be higher.

Programming Lead

Incorporating the design into the gameplay systems and ensuring code standards.

Initially I set out to mostly work on the design of the game. But after most of the game's design was decided on, I was asked by the other programmers to help out and take the lead since they struggled to figure out some of the architecture together. Through this process I was able to ensure that the design of the game was properly incorporated into the gameplay systems. I also made sure that code standards were met, namely architecture consistency and exception handling. This way it became easier for everyone working on and with the systems to use, expand, and maintain it.

Camera System

A static third person camera system that allows the designer to set a different camera angle when the player enters a new area. This way, the camera turns to specific angles that allows the player to see important gameplay aspects without having to learn how to move a camera.
