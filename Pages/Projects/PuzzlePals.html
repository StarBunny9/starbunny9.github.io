Project Description

Puzzle Pals is a game made to be displayed in the central library of Enschede and is made in collaboration with children from the International School Twente. They all made monster drawings and came up with various super powers for their monsters.

Puzzle Pals delivers an opportunity to explore the imagination of children and casually challenges the problem-solving skills of curious minds that are open to a short, positive, and brightening experience while exploring the library.

My Contributions
Design Direction

Since our team consisted of three programmers, one designer and two artists, I decided that this was a good opportunity to make some steps in learning proper game design. This also balanced out the roles within the team.

My role boils down to aligning and communicating different ideas with team members and external parties to create a cohesive experience for the children and anyone who plays the game. This also includes making decisions based on the requirements, scope, target audience, how the product is going to be used, playtests, and when it's not so clear what the best solutions are.

Programming Lead

I also made sure that code standards were met, namely architecture consistency and exception handling. This way it became easier for everyone working on and with the systems to use, expand, and maintain it.

Other
Camera System

There was a lot of discussion within our team on how we should handle the player camera. Internally, we preferred the player to be able to have a freely orbiting camera. But we noticed during playtests with the children, that they really struggled getting a grip on how to use such a camera system.

Considering the fact that the final product is supposed to be a short and lighthearted experience, we chose to continue with a static third person camera, that only moves when the player enters a new area, based on where important gameplay aspects are. This decision reflected positively from our playtests, where children struggled considerably less to find their way around the levels.

Allowing the player to learn a freely orbiting camera system would likely have been a better choice if the average playtime of a user would be higher.

The implementation is a very simple script that lerps the offset of the camera when the player enters a camera zone. Even though it is a simple change, it still had a strong effect on how the game was played!

FMOD and Sound System

I implemented a simple sounds system using FMOD to easily be able to play the necessary sounds without having to delve into how FMOD connects to Unity exactly. The Implementation is based on this video by Shaped by Rain Studios. Learning more about FMOD is still on the planning for future projects.

Controller Support

The game is meant to be played using a controller. Assigning the right buttons and joysticks to the right monster inputs was very easy using Unity's Input Action Assets. This way it is easy to listen to input events and subscribe these to the right methods. Though I did still have to figure out how to connect it to the UI properly. Which all worked smoothly in the end.

Visual Effects

I created the interactive Bubbles, wind stream, end of level portal, and the bill-boarding stars shader in the background. Simple but effective effects and I improved my Unity Particle system skills!

Player Movement

For the player movement we took a lot of inspiration from the original Skylands games, such as Spyro's Adventure.
